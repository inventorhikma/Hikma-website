Gambling

A Catalogue of Slippery Worlds

I’m done gambling my energy away.

There is a gamble even in the writing that I write. In the thoughts that I speak, in the words that I communicate with, in the choices that I make. I have these inconsistent thoughts in my mind and then I passively watch how they flow. When the situation arrives, I think it’s the only thing I’ve got, so I let it fly. I let my mouth, my decisions, fly without a second thought. Without giving the thought space. Without giving the words intention. Without giving the decisions direction or a goal. I just let it fly.

There’s this thing my mother says. She says a person like that cannot be held. It makes more sense in my native language. What she means is that a person like that is constantly throwing whatever they find in their life, in their world, at you—like it’s okay. Or they just keep throwing until something hits. Like a gambler, you know. You keep giving away what you have until you win, and then you get a high from what you win, and then you go chasing another high the next time.

Imagine if this is the pattern of your decision-making. If you’re gambling at every turn, that means you are gambling part of your soul to get something every time.

This isn’t just about money. Money is just one source of the object. The deeper root is the gambling away of your energy, your will. Your ability to stop and make a choice. To stop and not need to respond to the energy when you don’t have the answer.

For me, I used to pretend. That was my automatic reaction to almost anything. It was like my operating system ran on the need to make sure everything in reality was okay. That was the faulty image I had of what “okay” was. My emotional attachment to success made me believe that every reality I encountered—within myself and outside myself—needed to meet some success rating. Some bullseye. No matter how far into the clouds I was.

And so I would gamble my energy. I calibrated my energetic output to keep throwing energy at whatever was around me, no matter how foolish it became.

I want to take it easy with this story. I don’t want to gamble with it.

I hit a stop when I tried to write. I tried to gamble my lines, hoping that I’d hit the same emotional state I was in when I felt good in that first paragraph. But then I started looking around for what I could gamble. I tried to make sense of what the point of this story was. What I was trying to convey.

Not for the image. Not to meet that image of success. But to make sure I speak my truth.

And I suppose that is what this is, right? The point of this gambling story. To come to the truth and to be okay with whatever that truth is. Even if it is not a long story that looks like other articles out there. Just the real, unfiltered truth.

At least then, I wouldn’t squander energy I don’t know how to spend.
