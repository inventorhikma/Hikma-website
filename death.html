Death

Shadow Cartography

It was always about the ending
the bit of existence that scared me,
the part that never came for me.

Everything ends anyway,
and I cannot run.
Death is a cycle.
It always exists.
Maybe that is why my sister clung to it so tightly
because mother rejected it so completely.

She was given attention in excess,
too much to let go.
She wanted it to end.
I never got even the smallest piece I wanted.
I got rejection instead.

I did not want things to end.
Any attention I held on to
Things lingered.
Memories stacked in grief.
No witness reflected my pain.
No one saw me.
No one held me.

My grief ran endless,
scorching the depths of my desires.
I stayed.
I stayed in doors that should have closed.
Until the rooms themselves
mirrored my fear of endings.
The Fear of death.

I wonder what the first room was made of.
Anger? Sadness?
Perhaps it was the beatings from my father and brother
but no.
it was my mother’s neglect.

She made a man more important than me.
A man’s feelings mattered more.
A child’s needs abandoned, ignored, cold.

That first room
it formed all the others.
The door that contained
The endless well of grief.
I harbored

A baby left to self-soothe.
Fantasies and angels filling the space
to protect me from maternal absence.

No mirror to witness my grief.
My little sad desire.
Her silence.
Her disinterest.
Her inability to see me.
Left me with an open door
Not witnessed, not nurtured
to absorb all external grief.

I stayed alive for everyone else,
absorbing their desires,
bringing closure to their realities.
Animated.
Useful.
Reflective.

I tried to create justice
in my twisted conditioning.
Playing a painful role,
I became the death
that ended other people’s cycles.

I watched and endured
as all their poison poured.
Grief wallowed.
Grief expanded.
Grief repeated.

I stayed inside rooms
that should have closed.
Doors left ajar
until they became corridors,
until it became a mansion
built entirely from unfinished grief,
from all the endings I mirrored.

My life was extracted.
I slayed all their demons.
My energy kept draining.
I brought death for their progression
as I postponed my own endings.

I performed life as a shield,
absorbing what was never mine,
metabolizing grief in hopes
that I’d be given relief.

So I lingered.
Immortal in survival.
Exhausted by continuity.

I performed death so much
I feared the collapse of others
the silence after confrontation,
the emptiness that follows truth.

Death learned to fear her own death.
I thought if I could end things for them,
they wouldn’t end me.

Justice became my disguise.
Endurance became my proof.
Staying became my sentence.

But death was never meant
to stay forever.

Death is meant to end.
To close rooms.
To stop the cycle.
To rest.

Now I see it clearly:
I wasn’t afraid of death.
I was afraid of who would survive
if I finally let myself end.

So I stop.

I stop performing justice
inside a broken system.
I stop absorbing what was never mine.
I stop ending things for others
while my own grief lingers unpaid.

This is my ending.
The destruction
of all the rooms I feared closing,
completing a feat
I deemed as dangerous.

Death is permission.
Death is closure.
Death is the right to end.

I close the doors.
I stop staying.
I stop reflecting.
I claim my own life.
My own grief.
My own endings.

I let death die,
so I can finally live.
