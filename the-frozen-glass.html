The Frozen Glass

A Catalogue of Slippery Worlds

When I used to work at the bar, the way the night builds up is so interesting. It was a great time for experimentation while being sober. I used to be the only person who went in sober and left sober. I usually left feeling so high on life.

It’s because I could see how my energy interacted with people. As people got more and more drunk, they felt a lot freer in who they were. Less judgmental. Less concerned with everyone else. More involved within themselves. And as their energy changed, so did mine. It was almost as if, because everyone started feeling free, so did I. My energy realized there weren’t spectators. And because there were no spectators, I allowed myself to come out too. I allowed myself to feel comfortable in the space.

So the drinking they did became this trickling effect that allowed me to be myself entirely. You would see how I was and think to yourself, that girl must be really drunk. But usually, people were so involved within themselves that they weren’t judging me. I was just being me.

I think there are people who are able to allow different parts of themselves to come out while they’re sober. But I think there are plenty of people who are stuck behind that frozen glass of sobriety, where they need a crutch to be fully themselves.

Sometimes there are people we feel safe enough with to be who we truly are. It’s too complex of an energy to define entirely, but this is how I see it. The frozen glass forms and breaks depending on judgment, vulnerability, safety, or that crutch. We’re hiding parts of our personalities that are too traumatized, too protected, too accustomed to being kept inside. They need the right place to come out.

It’s also interesting when people meet. We peel layers and layers of ourselves slowly. And sometimes we can’t peel those layers because the frozen glass is still so intact that what lies underneath only comes out when we’re not sober. Sometimes some people raise our glasses up. It becomes this ego fight, where partaking in drinking or weed only allows what lies underneath to come out.

But then I wonder…

Was it ever the alcohol? Or was it simply permission?

Permission to stop performing. Permission to stop holding yourself together. Permission to stop watching yourself from the outside.

Maybe the drink doesn’t create another version of us. Maybe it just quiets the part that keeps knocking on the frozen glass, reminding us to stay inside.

I don’t think the goal is to rely on the thing that cracks it. I think the goal is to slowly find the places, the people, and eventually the relationship with yourself where the glass doesn’t need to exist at all.

Where you can walk into the room just as sober as you walked in, but still leave feeling high on life.
