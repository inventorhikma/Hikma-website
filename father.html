Father

Shadow Cartography

Appearance was the goal.
How it looked —
that’s how I learnt most things.
How do I show up
to meet what’s expected?
How should it look in the story,
even when it wasn’t true
to my own narrative?

Appear peaceful
when it was chaos.
A happy child
performing survival.

That short walk
I took every day
in the years before I left home —
I learned to carry courage quietly,
to appear as I should,
worshipping my provider.
Since I was subdued

My room was thru the living room.
My father sat in his chair,
arms crossed,

When I crossed the room
Watching my every move
Attention fixed on my movement
eyes fixed on the TV,
Yet never off me

If I forgot something,
I had to walk again
another round of silent hell.
His head shaking with disapproval
Each step made me feel smaller
Cutting thru me in a single glance
As i feel my worth collapse

So I learned to walk
without giving him satisfaction,
to disappear inside
while holding it together outside.

I became a mirror
A soft machine
reflecting an illusion of peace,
masking the ache within
Every glance reminding me
that I was not enough
A rebellion spurring inside
My stride growing more spiteful

Embodying the courage to keep walking
Bearing the judgment before my eyes,
Walking steady
while fighting to belong,
In the illusion of safety I performed

How brutal it was
to be a child under a gaze like that —
to be judged for existence

The torment makes sense now
why the ones I love most
torment me.
Because I appear
as they want me to.
But there’s always
that faint scent of illusion,
that small lack of emotion
revealing the pretense.
The harshness in my compromise
The karma of my self betrayal

I became the ghost of your desire,
convincing even myself
that what you saw
was truly me.
My essence went invisible,
fulfilling the story
others needed to see.

But now,
I see the wound of that walk
the way I was forced
to justify the innocent steps i walked
I pretended to have peace
to hide my lack of belonging
As i searched to find safety in your scrutiny

But that walk
taught me more than fear.
I learned to shape the chaos
into something I could survive
to bring order
where authority demanded obedience,
to serve my soul quietly
while my spirit burned beneath.

Since leaving home,
I’ve walked many invisible paths.
Attracting the same eyes
In different faces.
the ones who make belonging
a transaction of comfort.
The ones I disappear for,

Until I learned
to end the show —
to let the ship of your comfort sink,
to burn brightly, unlabeled,
free to stand
in any space.

I no longer need
to defend every step I take.
I can exist
without shrinking,
without covering my light
to soften your discomfort.

I walk honestly now —
unburdened
by the temptation of your judgment,
whole,
enough,
and seen,
belonging
to myself.
