The Space Between Two Kingdoms

A Catalogue of Slippery Worlds

This story is about space. You have space. And so do I. When we come together, the space doubles. The reality doubles. Your space does not become mine. Mine does not become yours. Your reality does not become mine. Mine does not become yours. They both exist at the same time. That is how it is supposed to be.

But this story is also about enmeshment. What happens when two spaces begin to dissolve into one another. When two realities begin to overlap. When the rooms we inhabit become shared rooms, and the gardens we tend begin to grow across one another’s borders.

Imagine there are two mansions. Two kingdoms. Two lands. Two corners of the universe. Two worlds. Two gardens. At first, there is a line between them. A border. A gate. A place where one kingdom ends and the other begins. But borders can blur. Sometimes slowly. Sometimes so gradually that neither person notices it happening. Until one day, there is one canvas instead of two. One mind. One life. One shared rhythm. Illusionary performances become habitual obligations. Mental prisons are blurred by fearful thoughts that are never acted upon, and by standing on insecure ground that neither can seem to leave. And neither can remember exactly where one began and the other ended.

Perhaps this is what happens when two opposite energies meet. Always leaning toward the extreme that your nature feels comfortable with. In this narrative there is a Yang and there is a Yin. One igniting in Fire and breathing Air. The other flowing in Water and grounding in Earth. Fire that burns without freedom. Air that feels every wind, with no filter between the world and the self. Water that drowns in the very depths it was meant to flow through. Earth that lingers in ground it cannot leave.

In the ways of health. In the ways of duality. If there is too much Yang, eventually something must pull toward Yin. And if there is too much Yin, eventually something must pull toward Yang. Perhaps that is the nature of the extremes. If one remains too long on one side, life itself begins pushing us toward the other. Or you’ll cease to exist.

And human beings are no different. We are enormous worlds contained inside tiny bodies. Sometimes, if the energetic shoe fits, we too can crash. You mesh with another person. If it mirrors. If it flows. If it feels like the exact kind of current you were meant to meet. The Yang extreme can enmesh with the Yin extreme. And maybe that is what we were.

Our energies showed up differently in the world. We inhabited space differently. One saw too much and the other felt too much. One knew how to make too much space, giving away her fire or overheating without expressing it. The other did not yet know how to share that space, caught inside an uncontrollable ocean current that flowed into every corner.

Especially when you are in different places of growth. Different levels of awareness. When life taught you different lessons. When trauma gave you different gifts. When the experiences we’ve had brought different wisdoms, different pains, different pleasures. And what if one is scared to come down and the other is scared to come up?

This story is from the lens of Yang’s universe. From the cards she was dealt in playing this game of life.

Yang pushed herself to expand her awareness. Maybe that was her nature. Or maybe that was what she needed to do to balance all the fire within her—to give it direction and find all of its expressions. All the patterns and images she was plagued with. She walked countless cycles, traveling through all the realms, chasing the insights she needed. She kept going to stay alive, until the insight became something she could pour into the world. Enough to generate life. To create in the material.

But she was limited by emotions. In the land of feeling. In the physical. The land of expressing. So honest in her nature, the ways of life could not bear her burns. Her conditioning suppressed an expression that burned too intensely for the world she had been taught to inhabit. Her fire was too piercing, her perception too revealing, and her expression too powerful to move freely without consequence.

And when the two of us met, our corners fell into one another.

The things Yang possessed were enough to power Yin’s growth. The things Yin carried were grounding enough to give Yang stairs to finally come down.

Yang gave Yin expansion. Perception. Language for things she could feel but could not yet name. She brought patterns into focus, opened doors to places Yin had never thought to look, and made possibility feel tangible. Yang’s mind gave movement to what Yin had only known as feeling.

And Yin gave Yang somewhere to land.

Her presence was grounding in a way Yang had rarely experienced. She pulled Yang out of the endless rooms of her mind and into something she could touch, feel, inhabit. She gave weight to Yang’s fire. A place for it to descend.

But what began as fulfilling could become too much.

Yang became blinded by the illusion of love. By the ground Yin so easily pulled her toward. By the harmony they seemed to create, even when that harmony sometimes disguised the absence of intentional feeling.

And perhaps what Yin experienced was the inverse.

What first felt like illumination could become exposure. What felt like possibility could become pressure. Yang saw too much, asked too much, felt too deeply, and moved toward places Yin was not always ready to enter. The same perception that gave Yin language could also make her feel seen before she was ready to be seen. The same fire that gave her warmth could become something she needed protection from.

Yang could feel like a door opening.

But sometimes Yin needed the door closed.

And Yang, in turn, lingered in places she did not want to remain. When someone who is so close to you lives in a different timeline than you, sometimes you remain in places that you do not belong.

She was too aware. She made too much space for realities she did not need to encounter. She stayed when she could have left. She stayed too long inside rooms that had already begun to suffocate her, convincing herself that staying was part of loving.

While Yang’s presence could feel like too much pressure for Yin, Yin’s world began to feel like too little truth for Yang. Too little movement. Too little need to remain in spaces that no longer held what was true for her.

Eventually, Yang would have to burn down the house that had become too contained.

But she was not ready yet.

A friendship built without enough lines created a shared space with no divided ownership. The same pace. The same rooms. Lingering where neither quite knew who the space belonged to.

Yang found herself waiting for Yin to catch up to realities Yang had already travelled. Ignoring her desire to keep moving. Believing the travels should be done together.

She mistakenly stopped in her tracks to give a lift.

She decided to go back.

She walked the same cycles that had already become well-learned lessons, sharing the tools from her garden, the wisdom from her universe, forgetting her own lane and forgetting where she had left off.

She kept walking a different path so she would not leave Yin behind.

She was afraid of what might happen to their connection if she continued toward her own becoming.

So she slowed down.

Then she stopped.

And somewhere in that decision, she stopped walking beside Yin and started walking around her.

Around her pain. Around her moods. Around her limits. Around what she could receive. Around what might overwhelm her.

Yang learned Yin’s weather so well that she forgot to check her own.

She began tending to Yin’s garden.

The hard work. The knowledge. Everything she had accumulated began feeding another land.

And the garden was prepared to receive it.

This is what enmeshment can do when it moves beyond the emotional and into the physical. The corners of two lives begin to overlap, and what belongs to one person’s universe starts taking root in another person’s soil.

You reap what you sow.

You harvest what you plant.

And meaning does not always determine the harvest.

Yang was sharing seeds she had found in her own garden. Ideas she had cultivated. Truths she had travelled through. Tools she had earned by walking her own cycles. But somewhere along the way, she began planting those seeds in someone else’s yard.

And they grew.

Flowers bloomed.

The soil became fertile.

The garden began producing things that had once only existed in Yang’s world.

And that was part of the danger.

Because Yang was still learning how to bring what she discovered in the spiritual world into the physical one. She was learning how to make the invisible tangible. How to take perception and turn it into creation. How to take an insight and give it form.

But now, some of what she had discovered was being made real somewhere else.

And in the world of the unseen, there was only one person who could see the whole truth of where those seeds had come from.

Yang.

The physical world could only see what had grown.

It could not see whose garden had first cultivated it.

Slowly, the garden became accustomed to its provider.

And Yang became so accustomed to tending another garden that she mistook the labor for love.

The habit became a routine. The routine became normal. And the normal became a life.

Perhaps this is where entitlement begins.

Not always with cruelty.

Sometimes with access that was never defined. With time that was always given. With energy that was always available. With a door that stayed open for so long that the person standing outside forgot there was a door at all.

What is continuously given without being named can eventually be mistaken for what is owed.

What is offered freely can become expected.

Yang stopped keeping track of her own while helping build someone else’s.

She kept building herself brick by brick, and somehow gave another person the authority to decide which bricks could remain. She built a wall and called it unnecessary. Built a room and apologized for taking up space. Built a garden and kept letting someone else move through it without asking. Built a house and then wondered why she felt homeless.

There is a particular kind of pain that comes from finally realizing that the person you kept making room for had been taking up the room you were supposed to inhabit.

I thought I was loving you.

I thought I was patient.

I thought I was understanding.

I thought I was giving you time to become who you were becoming.

I thought I was walking beside you while you found your way.

And somewhere in there, she saw the lines.

She saw where they had become skewered. Where Yin’s world had crossed into hers. Where hers had begun disappearing inside Yin’s. She saw herself beginning to vanish.

And she tried to build a wall.

But the wall was frightening.

Because Yang had learned that boundaries could cost her connection. The habits she had practiced for so long had become normal. What once required a conscious choice had become instinct. She had spent so long adjusting herself to the shared space that she no longer noticed how often she was the one adjusting.

So every time she felt the rain in her garden, she looked toward Yin’s first.

Is she okay?

Can she handle this?

Will this be too much?

Should I say it now?

Should I wait?

Should I make it softer?

Should I make more space?

And when I finally spoke about the things that hurt me—when I attempted to place lines where there were none—I was plagued by the fear of loss.

Or stopped by your emotions. Your fear of change. Your fear of seeing beyond what was right in front of you. Your lack of awareness that our image had become blurry, fused at the edges.

You had become accustomed to inhabiting a space that did not begin as yours, but that I had made comfortable enough for you to breathe in. And I had become accustomed to shrinking myself so you could understand the world we had built.

But you were still looking at it from inside the room.

I was beginning to see the house.

Our enmeshed rooms required me to explain every movement, because somewhere along the way I had started moving together with only one steering wheel. My movement no longer felt like mine if it affected the shared space. My decisions required translation. My boundaries required explanation. Even our definition required language for impressions that had never been given names.

We had become so fused that separating our realities began to feel like an accusation.

And somehow, my pain became another thing I had to manage.

You were hurt that I was hurt.

And suddenly, I was comforting you for the pain I had finally gathered the courage to name.

That was the part I could no longer ignore.

Because both realities existed.

Yin could be hurt.

And Yang could be hurt.

One did not erase the other. One did not become more real simply because it was spoken more loudly.

Because Yang was becoming someone too.

And she had kept asking her becoming to wait. She had waited for Yin to catch up. She had waited for the right moment. She had waited until the boundary could be understood. She had waited until changing would no longer hurt anyone.

But she finally saw what the waiting had cost her.

She had become a mental tool. A generator. A source of energy for another kingdom’s growth. Even three thousand miles away, she was still the fire and air that finished Yin’s water and earth. The Yang to Yin. The energy that invented in the mind while Yin brought it into the physical.

But the source was hers.

The blueprint was hers.

She had found it. She had planted it. She had created it.

For herself.

And somehow, they still could not separate their two mansions. Their two corners of the universe. Their two kingdoms had forgotten their borders.

And Yang began to understand something she had spent years refusing to see: she had been abandoning her own pain every time she made someone else’s pain more important than hers.

Her fire had always been too honest. Too piercing. Her conditioning had taught her to dull it, to reduce its heat so it would not burn the people around her.

And somewhere along the way, Yang had become an instrument for other people’s emotions.

She could feel them. Enter them. See the patterns inside them. Her fire illuminated what others were feeling, and her air gave it movement, language, perspective. People could bring their emotions to her and she could make sense of them.

She became so good at giving up her space to see what was happening inside everyone else that she often reached their feelings before she reached her own. She used her own body, her own house, to make every guest she encountered the main subject. She would stand outside her own house and observe.

Then she met Yin.

And Yin was water in an overwhelming volume.

There was so much to feel. So much to hold. So much asking to be understood, named, illuminated. Yin easily took up all the space Yang held. Yang’s fire and air were enormous; they could contain oceans. Yang was made to leave her house open for someone else’s inhabitation, to the point of erasure, that she no longer had a space of perception to look from.

Yin had filled the space Yang had spent years leaving open.

And that fullness finally forced Yang to see the emptiness she had left behind.

The water forced her into the ground she had spent so long avoiding. Into the rooms she had forsaken while tending to everyone else’s emotions. Into the parts of herself she had left uninhabited because there was always someone else’s pain waiting to be understood.

Yang was too used to being the fire that burned down other people’s rooms that she finally met her match. She met water that overfilled her to the point that it exploded her house.

Yin’s overwhelming inhabitation finally became the reason for Yang’s recognition. She finally pushed someone else out of a space she had forgotten was hers to inhabit.

She had to inhabit her own rooms. She had to make space for her own weather. She had to feel what was happening inside her before reaching for an explanation of what was happening inside someone else.

She brought her own energy back to the ground. Planted it in her own garden. Became audacious enough to harvest her own intense fruit in her own land. She inhabited her own rooms no matter who was watching. She was done living in rooms designed for someone else’s comfort. Done waiting for someone else to finish learning before she was allowed to continue becoming. Done shrinking her life into something another person could comfortably hold. She was done holding back her becoming until someone else understood it.

Because she finally understood: she did not need permission to change. She did not need agreement to build a boundary. She did not need someone else to understand the wall before she was allowed to build it.

Yang finally pulled the plug. I finally claimed my own kingdom.

I am the one who leads.

For I am the one who sees.

I place the boundaries.

I decide where the wall goes.

I decide when the door closes.

I will no longer hold myself back until you catch up.

I will no longer leave rooms unfinished because you are not ready to understand why I need them.

I will build them anyway.

I will inhabit spaces you cannot perceive. I will move through realities you cannot measure.

I will no longer hide the parts of me that cannot be understood.

I will change. I will move differently.

I will close what needs closing.

I will remove access where access no longer serves me.

I will let the consequence be whatever it is.

She finally understood that her perception was her gift. And her power. She was a different kind of leader. She decided to stop limiting herself for others’ comfort. She could live beyond the limits of someone else’s understanding. She could inhabit rooms no one else understood. She could enter spaces no one else could perceive. She could build things whose architecture could not yet be measured by another person’s eyes. She would no longer make her life legible at the expense of making it smaller. She would no longer choose only the rooms where someone else felt comfortable having her. She would take up space even where her existence could not be explained, where her intensity could not be contained, where her becoming could not yet be seen.

And if someone could not understand the room, they did not have to enter it. If they could not hold the intensity, they did not have to stand in the fire. She could keep living without needing their belief in her seeing.

Amidst all this realization, Yang finally saw what had been happening. The different forces playing within their energies.

Yang had spent her life perceiving.

Yin forced her to feel.

Yang had understood Yin as the one who needed insight for what she was feeling. But now Yang was catching up to Yin in a different way. Yin forced Yang to find grounding for what she was feeling. She was finally feeling the pain she had spent so long thinking through.

And perhaps that was when it all crashed.

Not into destruction.

But into an uncontrollable border.

The force required to repel what had become too entangled.

That was the Great Collision.

The two kingdoms that had forgotten where they ended finally generated enough force to separate. Not because one kingdom had to disappear, but because they both had to exist.

They had each reached the far end of the other’s extreme. Each had become enough of the other to generate their own Yang and Yin. And for the first time, the space between them could exist again.

And maybe that space was never the enemy.

Perhaps it was what allowed both kingdoms to remain kingdoms amidst their convergence.

A border did not mean one had rejected the other. A closed door did not mean the room had ceased to exist. Distance did not mean the connection had been meaningless.

It meant there was somewhere to stand.

Somewhere to return to.

A place where one could say:

This is mine.

And this is yours.

And now I want to know what remains when I don’t reduce myself and occupy my own space.

What remains when I stop carrying a relationship through my over-functioning? When I stop adjusting? When I stop making myself smaller? When I stop leaving my own space for another? When I stop being the bridge. The generator. The fire that keeps another kingdom warm.

Maybe the kingdom stands on its own. Maybe it doesn’t. Maybe some rooms go dark. Maybe some doors never open again.

Maybe that is the consequence.

But I would rather know what remains than keep myself alive inside something I have been carrying. Or remaining in rooms that I am too big to fit in.

And perhaps there is another lens to this story.

Because this was never simply Yang against Yin.

It was two extremes meeting. A duality with its own polarity. Two forces that found one another because each carried something the other had not yet learned to inhabit.

Perhaps neither lesson was meant to require the other kingdom to disappear. Perhaps the lesson was always supposed to return them to themselves. Perhaps I did not trust that you could hold my visibility. Perhaps I did not trust that you could stand beside what I was becoming without asking me to make myself smaller. Perhaps I had mistaken being understood for being allowed to exist.

I don’t need to do that anymore.

If my becoming is not perceptible to whoever is watching, I will not make it smaller so I can be accepted.

Because I finally understand: I was never asking for too much. I was giving too much power to spaces that asked me to disappear.

I will leave the spaces where I have lingered and return to the ones where I can breathe. Perhaps that is why I could never fully inhabit my own kingdom. I kept choosing the rooms that made others comfortable and abandoning the ones where I was most visible. I learned to fit myself into spaces that could contain me, even when they could not hold me.

And in doing so, I was prolonging my own becoming.

Perhaps that is the difference between love and enmeshment. Love can stand at the border. Love can see the other kingdom and still leave it sovereign.

And perhaps I should have known sooner that I was not being asked to become smaller. I was simply standing in a place where my size was inconvenient. So I am leaving the small spaces I cannot fit into anymore. The spaces I cannot breathe in. The spaces I cannot grow in.

I do not need you to understand. I do not need you to agree. I do not need to prove that my kingdom is real.

I finally remembered what is mine. And I finally remembered that I can be so much more.

That I carry emotions others may never know how to hold. That I see things others may never see. That there are people I have yet to meet who will not require me to translate myself before they can respect me. People who can stand beside what I carry without asking me to put it down.

So I will stop restricting my freedom to make someone else comfortable.

I will inhabit my rooms.

I will tend to my garden.

I will let my fire burn at its own intensity.

I will let my becoming become.

Because I am tired of being silent. I am tired of being invisible. I am tired of feeling lonely inside myself. I am tired of living on the edge of my own kingdom. And most of all—

I am tired of dealing with overbearing, entitled assholes simply because I remained where I no longer belonged.
